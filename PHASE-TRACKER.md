## Open gates — named, not buried

1. **Phase 0 rung 2 — hours calibration.** ~52h is an agent's estimate. The playbook: *"the agents
   proposing it are structurally blind to duration… PASS = an hours estimate or Oscar's calibration
   is written down. Absent = UNMEASURABLE = fail."* **Oscar must write a number.**
2. **Phase 3 — design owner unnamed.** The submission artifact is a 4-minute film of a screen, so
   the playbook requires a named design owner and a `/design` run. Neither exists. **This is the
   Phase-5 failure mode in advance:** the exhibit was advice rather than a gate, so it got cut as
   polish — *"that is the entire Overturn loss."*
3. **Phase 6 is not delegable.** Oscar drives the degraded path himself. Put it in the calendar now,
   not on Aug 30.

## Lessons already applied from the playbook
- **Phase 0 exists because the decision is the expensive part** (RETRACT: 54 commits, the first one
  already carried the whole thesis). The three killed ideas are recorded rather than re-argued.
- **Phase 1 exists because KYA died on a rule knowable in hour one.** Every constraint is quoted.
- **Nothing runs ahead of the thing that can fail the submission** — slice 0 is the Cloud Run deploy.
- **The scoper cannot feel their own scope's smallness**, same as the builder cannot see their own
  demo's bugs. Cursor and an outside review are aimed at the scope, not just the code.
